Precision, recall and F1

A reference for the story-atlas genre labeling. All three numbers run from 0 to 1, and higher is better.

The three counts everything is built from

A method (a model, or a database lookup) gives each work a list of genres. You have judged genres for the same work. Compare the two lists and every genre lands in one of four places:

NameWhat it isIn the blind check
HitThe method gave the genre, and it is right.The method gave it and you starred it.
ErrorThe method gave the genre, and it is wrong.The method gave it and you marked it Wrong.
MissThe genre is right, and the method did not give it.You starred it and the method did not give it.
NeutralAcceptable but not central. Counted nowhere.The method gave it and you left it blank.

The counts are added up over all the works first, and the three numbers are computed once from those totals. (That pooling is called micro-averaging. The alternative, computing a score per work and averaging the scores, would let a work with one genre weigh as much as a work with five.)

Precision

Of the genres the method gave, what share were right?

precision = hits / (hits + errors)

It punishes saying too much. A method that gives only the one genre it is surest of gets high precision.

Recall

Of the genres that are right, what share did the method give?

recall = hits / (hits + misses)

It punishes saying too little. A method that gives every genre on the list gets recall of 1.

The two have the same top and different bottoms. Precision divides the hits by everything the method said. Recall divides the hits by everything that was true.

F1

One number that is high only when precision and recall are both high.

F1 = 2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall)

The same thing written with the counts, which is often easier to think with:

F1 = 2 × hits / (2 × hits + errors + misses)

  • F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. An ordinary average of 1.0 and 0.0 is 0.5; their harmonic mean is 0. So a method can't buy a good F1 by maxing one number and abandoning the other.
  • When precision and recall are equal, F1 equals them. When they differ, F1 sits between them, closer to the lower one.
  • In the second formula an error and a miss cost exactly the same. That is what the "1" means: recall is weighted 1 times as heavily as precision. (F2 would count a miss as worse than an error; F0.5 the reverse.)

Reading a result quickly

  • High precision, low recall: a cautious method. What it says is right, but it leaves real genres out.
  • Low precision, high recall: a generous method. It finds the real genres and adds wrong ones.
  • Coverage (also in our tables) is separate: the share of works the method gave any answer for at all.
  • "Points" in our discussions means hundredths: 0.744 against 0.688 is a gap of 5.6 points.
